Why build it

India needs four things at once. No open system does all four.

Every big lab is building voice agents. The open ones each solve part of the problem. Formant is an attempt to solve all of it in one system, and to publish everything needed to reproduce it.

Indian languages and code-mixing

English and five major Indian languages plus Hinglish, with switches mid-sentence, and Indian names and numbers said the way people say them.

Full-duplex conversation

Listens while speaking. Says "haan" while you talk, waits through your pauses, and stops when you cut in.

Replies that make sense

A separate thinker model decides what to say, so the voice is fluent and the answer is complete and on topic.

Offline on cheap hardware

No internet and no GPU. If it runs on a 2-core laptop, it has headroom on any edge chip or phone.

Why so many hours of plain speech?

In September 2026 a developer trained a 600M Moshi-style duplex model, the same active size as Formant's talker, on about 100 hours of dialogue. The text side learned fast. The voice did not: the fine acoustic detail stayed under-trained and the audio never decoded cleanly. So Formant learns to speak from thousands of hours first, and learns conversation second. How it works

Two models. One keeps time, one does the thinking.

Models that hear and speak in one network are fluent but weak at reasoning. Strong language models are too slow to keep conversational timing. Formant splits the job, and runs each half on the part of the laptop that suits it.

Talker · the ears and voice

Owns the timing.

A small mixture-of-experts model, about 1.4 billion parameters with 0.5 billion active per frame, that never stops running. It hears every frame, keeps a live transcript, and decides when to speak, backchannel or stop.

  • Runs on the laptop's built-in Intel graphics, away from the thinker
  • Writes a running text of what you said, plus a turn signal
  • Covers short delays with natural fillers like "haan, ek second…"
Thinker · the brain

Owns the content.

A mixture-of-experts language model. It holds a lot of knowledge in RAM but reads only a small part of it for each word, which is what makes it fit this laptop.

  • Starts drafting while you are still speaking
  • Needs only ~5 tokens a second, the speed of speech
  • Candidates: Sarvam 30B, then a language slice of the course's 120B model
  1. ListenEvery 80 ms the talker reads one frame of your voice and one of its own.
  2. Track the turnIt labels the moment: speaking, paused, finished or interrupting.
  3. Draft earlyThe thinker reads your words as they arrive and starts a reply before you finish.
  4. CommitWhen the talker sees a real turn end, the thinker's draft becomes the reply.
  5. SpeakThe plan streams into the talker, which says it with natural timing and tone.
  6. YieldIf you cut in, the talker stops within a few frames and the thinker drops the rest.
Inside the laptop

The hard part is memory speed. Not memory size.

Each word a model generates means reading its weights from RAM. On this laptop that read runs at about 21 GB per second, roughly a fifth of an edge AI chip. Everything in Formant is sized to that number.
